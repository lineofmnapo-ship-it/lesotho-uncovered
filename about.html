MORIJA

Morija is where Lesotho’s modern history meets its culture. French missionaries of the Paris Evangelical Missionary Society founded a station here in 1833, and the town became an important centre of education and printing. The Morija Museum and Archives houses a rich collection of Basotho artefacts, historical documents and fossils, and nearby you can see dinosaur footprints and visit the old mission buildings. Each year the Morija Arts and Cultural Festival fills the town with traditional music, dance, food and crafts, and it is one of the best opportunities to experience living Basotho culture.

KOME CAVE DWELLINGS

Kome Cave Dwellings in the Berea district are homes built into overhanging sandstone cliffs, with mud walls and thatch shaping the entrances. Families have lived here since the nineteenth century, and some still do. Visiting with a local guide offers an intimate look at traditional Basotho life, with stories about how the community used the caves for shelter and protection.

DINOSAUR FOOTPRINTS

Dinosaur footprints are scattered across Lesotho’s sandstone, preserved from about 200 million years ago when the region was a very different landscape. Sites near Morija and at Moyeni (Quthing) have clear tracks of several species, and local guides can point out the best prints and explain what kind of animals made them.
